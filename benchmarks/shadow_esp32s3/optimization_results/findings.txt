ESP32-S3 shadow optimization and flash-gradient measurements

Measured on the USB-attached Makerfabs 5-inch 800x480 RGB565 display, at zoom100
and 240MHz CPU. Two complete runs per variant, seven warmed batches per run.
The side and corner tables preserve exact 8-bit alpha and RGB565 output.

These are gradient-data bytes; every mode also has the original 512 alpha-LUT bytes.
At zoom100 the complete linked firmware grows by 1,176 bytes for sides
or 48,276 bytes for sides plus corners, including code/alignment.
Static RAM is identical across variants (30,776 bytes); Decoration stays 84 bytes.
All tables are in flash DROM. No per-widget bitmap or RAM gradient copy is used.

All six radii use Scaled(dp) at the configured zoom. Level0 needs no gradient;
levels1..5 have independent profiles. Left and right share mirrored data; top
and bottom differ because the two shadow layers have vertical offsets. Other
radii retain the exact calculation. The cache-miss path reuses geometry rather
than recalculating it. dispatch_initial/ preserves the slower first attempt.

All three final optimized renderers match 64 original two-LUT raster captures
byte for byte. The eight cache/zoom tests and composition/legacy regressions pass.
RGB565 profiles on white, light gray and dark gray backgrounds also match exactly.
Caching changes speed and storage, not softness, quantization, or banding.

Treat these as warmed scene measurements. Cold-cache and heterogeneous scene
pressure while streaming unrelated images were not measured. Flat level0 timing
also changes slightly with firmware layout; small full-refresh differences are
not a guarantee that one cache setting will win in every application.

For this board and these scenes, use double_sides as the first choice: only 975
extra data bytes, 14-24% less full-refresh time over the optimized calculation
for the six requested radii. Relative to unoptimized double_flash, the radius16
example uses 28.6% less time. Side caching does not win isolated sampling: its
radius16 fixed-pixel cost is 5.0% higher, so choose based on the actual paint path.

The extra corner storage is hard to justify from these full-refresh results.
It improves isolated/tiled raster throughput, but full-refresh time is slightly
worse than sides alone at radii4..20 and only 0.5% better at radius24. The uncached
radius28 circle is 5.9% slower than sides alone; its fixed-pixel cost is 17.0%
higher than the calculation baseline. Keep corner caching opt-in and benchmark
it with the target application's workload. The board is left on double_sides.
